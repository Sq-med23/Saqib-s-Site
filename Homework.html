The article explains that about one in ten women die during childbirth, which portrays how severe the crisis is and why it demands global attention. Instead of only giving statistics, the article focuses on the personal stories of four women named Memunatu, Heela, Kadiatu, and Tetteh, allowing readers to better understand the emotional and physical realities behind the numbers. A major conflict discussed is the lack of proper healthcare infrastructure, as many women must travel long distances to reach hospitals without reliable transportation, which can delay critical care during emergencies and increase the risk of death. This conflict is made worse by the lasting effects of past health crises such as the “Ebola outbreak”, as well as ongoing challenges such as the, “COVID-19 pandemic”, both of which have weakened the healthcare system and made access to care even more difficult. To add on, healthcare issues, poverty and social conditions play a significant role in the crisis, as many women lack financial resources, education, and access to basic services, preventing them from receiving proper prenatal care or understanding the risks of pregnancy. Furthermore, the women featured in the article often have little control over their circumstances, which adds to their vulnerability and makes it harder for them to seek help or make informed decisions. The personal stories reveal the emotional toll of this crisis, including the loss of children and long term health issues, while also showing the resilience and determination of these women as they try to protect themselves and their families despite overwhelming challenges. Their experiences demonstrate that being a mother in Sierra Leone is not just a personal responsibility but a struggle shaped by larger social, economic, and political systems that limit opportunities and access to care. Overall, the article argues that the crisis of motherhood is a serious global issue that requires immediate attention and action from governments and international organizations, emphasizing that improving healthcare systems, increasing access to medical services, and addressing poverty are essential steps toward reducing maternal mortality and ensuring safer conditions for women.
